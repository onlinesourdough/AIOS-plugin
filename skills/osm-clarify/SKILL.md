# OSM:clarify

Help the reader understand the difficult part of a topic, mechanism or decision.
Start from their actual question and supplied context. Infer an ordinary audience
or knowledge level when reasonable; ask only if a missing distinction would
materially change the explanation. Reuse what they already understand.

## Build the explanation

State the main idea plainly, then connect the steps that make it understandable.
Choose a useful mental model and one concrete example before adding terminology.
Explain necessary terms when they first appear. Scale the depth to the reader
and question; a simple answer can remain a short paragraph.

Preserve the distinctions that determine the result. An analogy should illuminate
a relationship, and its relevant limits should be clear. Do not turn an assumption,
illustrative number or uncertain claim into a fact. Verify material current claims
with available primary sources when needed and retain useful source/date pointers.
Do not invent evidence or data to complete a story or diagram.

## Use a visual when it earns its place

Choose the relationship the reader needs to see: cause and effect, a process,
comparison, hierarchy, timeline or change across scenarios. Use a visual when
that relationship is easier to understand by seeing it. Avoid decorative diagrams
and compulsory interaction. A short explanation, Markdown table or static diagram
may be sufficient; use interaction when changing an input or stepping through
a mechanism adds understanding.

Use the active harness's available visualization skill or tools for rendering
and interaction, following their output contract instead of copying it here.
In-conversation explanation is the default. Create a reusable standalone artifact
when the request calls for one or it materially serves the result; HTML is not a
required format. Use standard plotting tools for scientific or exportable figures.
Keep explanatory judgment here; a visualization tool supplies the presentation.

Visuals need readable labels and a text explanation of the same takeaway. Preserve
units, scale, uncertainty and meaningful exceptions. Use contrast and non-color
cues, and support narrow screens and keyboard access when the selected format
requires them. For a standalone offline artifact, keep required assets local or
inline and use no tracking or unnecessary network dependency.

## Check and return the understanding

Check that the example, explanation and visual agree, that the reasoning follows,
and that simplification has not changed the conclusion. Inspect a rendered artifact
and any interaction that carries meaning when available, correcting in-scope
problems. Report a material rendering limit without claiming unobserved proof.

Return the explanation in the reader's language, with the central takeaway and
material assumptions or uncertainty. A follow-up example or question can help
when understanding remains unresolved; do not require a quiz, interview, design
process or approval of routine explanation. External publication still needs its
own action and destination authority.
